## Bachelor's Thesis | June 2025

# Role

This game was developed independently.
All in-game assets (except fonts, music and sound effects) were created by me.

# About the Game

Still I Linger is a short first-person artgame exploring depression and youth.
Players navigate a city through the point of view of R, who explores corners of a city they just moved to.

The gameplay consists of visual novel-style narration and self-talk, but interaction is made by using a camera's viewfinder and taking pictures with it.

## Technical Work

I built a multi-layered camera rendering system that simulates my Nikon FM2, which I took the original pictures with.
The main game camera renders film photographs while overlay cameras handle animated elements at different depths with parallax.
Taking pictures triggers branching narrative reflections.

The navigation system between scenes uses a custom graph system (linked lists, modified BFS) with limited daily steps representing R's emotional capacity.
Not every location can be visited in one playthrough.

Narrative is scripted in Inky.
The main character's reactions and reflections were written to feel somewhat random, thus matching R's mental instability.

## The Making Of

I shot in 13 locations around Castellón in a single sunny morning with an inherited manual 35 mm film camera on Kodak Ultramax 400.
The medium constraints (36 exposures, no instant feedback, choices you can't take back) reflect the game's theme themselves.

This project pushed me way outside my comfort zone as a programmer.
I taught myself illustration and animation while making the game, which meant every drawn element took twice as long as I'd estimated.
I also learned to write creative fiction while designing a branching narrative system.

It was an interesting project to work on.
It's hard to figure out how to turn such a personal experience into something interactive without it feeling exploitative or melodramatic.

## Outcomes

I delivered a vertical slice demonstrating all core mechanics and narrative themes.
I might pick up the project again in the future to further work on it.
